Cells & Ecosystem Dynamics

The Building Blocks of Life

1. Cellular Biology

  • Nucleus: The central control center of the cell; responsible for storing DNA and giving instructions. [cite: 1]
  • Mitochondria: The "powerhouse"; generates cellular energy (ATP) through respiration. [cite: 1]
  • Chloroplasts & Support: Plant cells have a rigid Cell Wall for support and Chloroplasts which perform the vital biological reaction of photosynthesis; animal cells do not. [cite: 1]
  • Hierarchy: Organisms build up in this exact biological hierarchy: Cell → Tissue → Organ → System → Organism. [cite: 1]

2. Ecosystems & Energy

  • Producers: Autotrophs create their own food using sunlight through photosynthesis. [cite: 1]
  • Decomposers: Organisms that break down dead organic matter to recycle essential nutrients back into the ecosystem. [cite: 1]
  • Food Webs: The arrows in a food chain always represent the Flow of Energy (pointing to the eater). [cite: 1]
  • Trophic Cascades: If an apex predator is removed from an environment, herbivores will overconsume producers, disrupting the balance. [cite: 1]

Rock & Water Cycles

Earth's Dynamic Systems

1. The Rock Cycle

  • Igneous Rock: Formed when molten magma or lava cools down and solidifies. [cite: 1]
  • Sedimentary Rock: Created via weathering and deposition. This rock type is the most likely to contain preserved fossil evidence. [cite: 1]
  • Metamorphic Rock: Formed when existing sedimentary (or igneous) rocks face intense underground heat and pressure. [cite: 1]
  • Weathering: The mechanical or chemical breakdown of rock into smaller fragments. [cite: 1]

2. The Water Cycle

  • Solar Energy: The Sun is the primary energy source driving the entire global water cycle. [cite: 1]
  • Evaporation: Liquid water turns to vapor driven by heat. Because of high surface evaporation, air near coastal areas (like Jeddah on the Red Sea) is more humid. [cite: 1]
  • Transpiration: The specific process where water is released directly from plant leaves into the atmosphere. [cite: 1]
  • Condensation: Water vapor cooling to form liquid droplets (creating clouds) in the upper atmosphere. [cite: 1]

Net Force & Energy Forms

Physics in Motion

1. Forces and Motion

  • Newtons (N): The standard scientific SI unit for measuring force. [cite: 1]
  • Net Force: 15 N Right and 5 N Left results in a Net Force of 10 N Right. Two forces pushing 20 N East and 30 N East combine to 50 N East. [cite: 1]
  • Balanced vs Unbalanced: When net force is exactly 0 N, forces are balanced. An unbalanced net force always causes an object to accelerate (change velocity). [cite: 1]
  • Friction & Inertia: Friction is the force that opposes sliding motion between contacting surfaces. Newton's First Law is also known as the Law of Inertia. [cite: 1]

2. Forms of Energy

  • Potential Energy: Stored energy based strictly on an object's position or elevation. [cite: 1]
  • The Pendulum: At its highest release point, it has Max Potential Energy and zero kinetic. At the lowest, fastest point, it possesses Max Kinetic Energy. [cite: 1]

Atomic Structure & Matter

The Quantum World

1. Inside the Atom

  • Protons & Neutrons: Protons carry a positive charge. Neutrons carry a neutral (zero) charge. Both reside packed together perfectly inside the central nucleus. [cite: 1]
  • Electrons: Carry a negative charge. They are located in electron shells (orbitals) outside the nucleus. [cite: 1]
  • Shell Limits: The inner-most electron shell can fit a maximum of 2 electrons. [cite: 1]
  • Neutral Atoms: In a neutral (uncharged) atom, the number of negative electrons exactly equals the number of positive protons. [cite: 1]

2. Periodic Table Math

  • Atomic Number: The atomic number of an element is always equal to the number of protons. [cite: 1]
  • Calculating Neutrons: Mass Number minus Atomic Number. (e.g., Lithium: Mass 7 - Atomic 3 = 4 Neutrons). [cite: 1]
  • Isotopes: These are atoms of the exact same element that have different numbers of neutrons. [cite: 1]

Biochemistry & Inquiry

The Chemistry of Life

1. Chemical Foundations

  • Elements of Life: Approximately 96% of all living matter is made up of just four elements: Carbon, Hydrogen, Oxygen, and Nitrogen. [cite: 2]
  • Water's Polarity: Water is an essential polar molecule because of unequal electron sharing. [cite: 2] This gives it properties like cohesion and adhesion, which allow water to move upward against gravity in plant xylem. [cite: 2]
  • pH Scale: A solution with a pH of 3.0 is classified as strongly acidic. [cite: 2]

2. Macromolecules & Enzymes

  • Lipids & Carbs: Lipids (fats) function primarily as the cell's long-term energy reserve. [cite: 2] Glucose (C6H12O6) belongs to the carbohydrate class of macromolecules. [cite: 2]
  • Proteins: Amino acids are the monomer building blocks of proteins, joined together in a polypeptide chain by peptide bonds. [cite: 2]
  • Enzymes: Biological enzymes act as catalysts by lowering the activation energy required for a reaction. [cite: 2]
  • Nucleic Acids: The nucleotide is the monomer unit that builds both DNA and RNA molecules. [cite: 2]

Ecosystems & Energy Flow

Energy Dynamics in Nature

1. Energy Transfer

  • Producers: Primary Producers (Autotrophs) synthesize organic biomass using energy from sunlight, which is the ultimate source of energy for nearly all terrestrial ecosystems. [cite: 2]
  • The 10% Rule: Only about 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next. [cite: 2] If producers have 10,000 Joules, only 100 J will reach secondary consumers. [cite: 2]
  • Energy Loss: The 90% of energy that is NOT transferred to the next level is lost to the environment as metabolic heat. [cite: 2]

2. Community Interactions

  • Food Webs: A diagram showing interconnected feeding pathways across an entire community is called a Food Web. [cite: 2]
  • Decomposers: Fungi and bacteria act as decomposers, recycling organic nutrients back into the abiotic soil. [cite: 2]
  • Symbiosis: Parasitism is a symbiotic relationship where one species benefits while the other is harmed. [cite: 2]
  • Keystone & Capacity: A Keystone Species plays a disproportionately large role in maintaining community structure. [cite: 2] The environment's maximum sustainable population is its Carrying Capacity. [cite: 2]

Cell Biology & Genetics

The Instructions of Life

1. Cell Structures

  • Eukaryotic Cells: The major feature that distinguishes eukaryotic cells from prokaryotes is the presence of a membrane-bound nucleus. [cite: 2]
  • Mitochondria: This organelle is the site of aerobic ATP generation through cellular respiration. [cite: 2]
  • Chloroplasts: Contains chlorophyll to convert solar photons directly into chemical glucose. [cite: 2]
  • Membranes & Ribosomes: The phospholipid membrane regulates the selective entry and exit of molecules. [cite: 2] Ribosomes synthesize polypeptide chains from mRNA transcripts. [cite: 2]

2. Genetics & Heredity

  • Base Pairing: In DNA rules, Adenine (A) always pairs perfectly with Thymine (T). [cite: 2]
  • Traits: A phenotype is an individual's observable physical expression of an inherited trait. [cite: 2] Alternate variations of the same gene are called alleles. [cite: 2]
  • Inheritance: A monohybrid cross between two heterozygous (Bb x Bb) parents results in a 3 dominant : 1 recessive phenotypic ratio. [cite: 2]
  • Mitosis: This type of cell division produces two genetically identical diploid somatic cells. [cite: 2]

Biology Diagnostic Check

Cellular Mechanics & Theories

1. Cellular Processes

  • Transport: Osmosis is the movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane down its gradient. [cite: 2] Active Transport requires direct metabolic energy (ATP) to pump solutes. [cite: 2]
  • Respiration: Aerobic cellular respiration is represented by: C6H12O6 + 6O2 -> 6CO2 + 6H2O + ATP. [cite: 2]
  • Enzymes: The Active Site is the physical location on an enzyme where specific substrate molecules bind. [cite: 2]
  • Cell Theory: The biological dogma stating that all living things are composed of cells is known as the Cell Theory. [cite: 2]

2. Genetics & Organization

  • Meiosis: The specific process that produces haploid gametes containing 23 chromosomes in humans. [cite: 2]
  • RNA vs DNA: Uracil (U) is the nitrogenous base present in RNA but completely absent in DNA. [cite: 2] A sudden change in a DNA nucleotide sequence is called a Genetic Mutation. [cite: 2]
  • Organization & Diet: In the hierarchy of life, an 'Organ' is directly above 'Tissue'. [cite: 2] Organisms feeding exclusively on autotrophic plant matter are herbivores. [cite: 2]

Diagnostic Analysis & Ecology

Ecological Systems & Planning

1. Ecological Concepts

  • Ecology: Formally defined as the scientific study of interactions with the environment. [cite: 2] An ecosystem encompasses both the biological community AND the abiotic environment. [cite: 2]
  • Niches & Biomes: An Ecological Niche is an organism's specific functional role and habitat. [cite: 2] The Arid Desert biome is characterized by low precipitation and drought-tolerant succulents. [cite: 2]
